Custom Tailoring

From first vision to final stitch — our five-step artisan process ensures your wedding attire is a true reflection of who you are.

Step One

Choose Your Design

Browse our signature lookbook and sample collection. Our consultants understand your event, your personality, and the aesthetic you're aiming for — then guide you to the designs that will make you feel extraordinary.

Step Two

Select Your Fabric

Choose from our curated library of imported and local fabrics — Banarasi silk brocades, Italian velvet, premium organza, and more. We'll help you match fabric weight and colour to your occasion and season.

Step Three

Precision Measurements

Our master tailor takes over 22 measurements to map your body perfectly. For overseas clients, we provide a detailed self-measurement guide with video support — ensuring a precise fit from afar.

Your completed ensemble is steam-pressed, inspected, and presented in our signature packaging. Delivery to your door, or collected in our boutique — your wedding wardrobe, complete and perfect.

Who We Are

Born in Sialkot,
Trusted Across Pakistan & Beyond

BinDawood Dulha House began as a single family workshop tucked in the heart of Dara Arraian, Sialkot — a city long celebrated for its craft and precision. What started as a neighbourhood tailoring house became, over generations, one of the region's most respected names in luxury groom wear.

We believe that a wedding is not merely an occasion — it is a chapter in a family's story. The groom's attire carries the weight of that moment. It must be worthy of the memory. That conviction drives every decision we make, from the thread we source to the final press before delivery.

Today BinDawood dresses grooms from Sialkot to Birmingham, from Lahore to Toronto, and partners with international importers seeking authentic, fully customisable groom wear for their own markets. Our collection spans ivory-embroidered sherwanis, jewelled prince coats, brocade jodhpuris, and everything the complete dulha needs — all crafted in-house by master karigars who have devoted their lives to this art.

Master Artisans

The Karigar's Hands

Every Bead, Placed
by Someone Who Cares

Behind every finished sherwani is a karigar who has spent years — sometimes decades — learning to place a single bead exactly where it belongs. There is no machine shortcut for this. Dense zardozi work like this can take a single pair of hands several days to complete, stitch by stitch, under the same kind of light their teachers worked under.

We don't rush that. A wedding happens once. The work that goes into the outfit should feel like it understands that.

Three Generations.
One Stitch.

What began as a single family workshop in Dara Arraian is now run by the grandchildren of the men who first opened its doors — the same care, the same patience, carried forward one wedding at a time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before your visit or order. Still have a question? WhatsApp us anytime.

Do you offer custom stitching and tailoring? +

Yes — custom tailoring is at the heart of what we do. Every garment can be made to your exact measurements, preferred fabric, and design specifications. Our master tailors have decades of experience crafting bespoke groom wear for all occasions.

How long does tailoring take? +

Standard custom orders typically take 2–4 weeks depending on complexity and embroidery requirements. For simpler designs, we can often complete in 1–2 weeks. We also offer an express service for urgent orders — please WhatsApp us to discuss your timeline.

Can Overseas Pakistanis place orders? +

How to Measure Yourself

Can't visit us in person? Follow these steps with a soft measuring tape, or skip this entirely and confirm measurements with our team over a WhatsApp video call.

01

Chest

Wrap the tape around the fullest part of your chest, under the arms and across the shoulder blades. Keep the tape level and snug but not tight — breathe normally.

02

Waist

Measure around your natural waistline — roughly at the belly button — keeping the tape parallel to the floor.

03

Shoulder Width

Measure straight across the back from the edge of one shoulder to the edge of the other, where the shoulder seam would sit.

04

Sleeve Length

With your arm slightly bent, measure from the shoulder edge down to just past your wrist bone.

05

Garment Length

Measure from the base of your neck (back) straight down to where you'd like the garment to end — typically knee-length for sherwanis.

06

Neck

Wrap the tape around the base of your neck, where a collar would naturally sit — leave a finger's width of slack.

Tips for Accurate Measurements

  • Measure over light, fitted clothing — not over a heavy jacket or bulky sweater.
  • Ask someone else to help if possible; self-measuring the back and shoulders is harder to do accurately alone.
  • Keep the tape snug but never pulled tight — you should be able to slide a finger underneath.
  • Stand naturally with arms relaxed at your sides, not stretched or hunched.
  • Still unsure? Send us your measurements over WhatsApp and our tailors will sanity-check them against your size before we cut any fabric.
